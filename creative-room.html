🍁 Leaf Rubbing Art Fall

Ages 3–12

Collect a few fallen leaves on a walk, place them under a sheet of paper, and rub a crayon sideways across the surface to reveal each leaf's veins and shape. A simple way to explore texture, patterns, and the outdoors together.

🎃 Pumpkin Patch Counting Fall

Ages 3–5

Using mini pumpkins (real or drawn), practice counting, sorting by size, or simple addition — "if we have 3 big pumpkins and 2 small ones, how many altogether?" A hands-on way to mix seasonal fun with early math.

Shadow Puppet Theater 🖨️ Printable

Ages 5–12

Make paper puppets and bring a story to life with light and shadows.

Download Puppet Cutouts Download Theater Instructions

What you’ll need

Printed puppet cutouts, scissors, tape, craft sticks, a flashlight, and a light-colored wall.

How to play

  1. Cut out the puppets with grown-up help and tape each one to a craft stick.
  2. Point the flashlight at the wall and hold a puppet between the light and the wall.
  3. Move the puppet closer to and farther from the light. Watch how its shadow changes.
  4. Take turns moving the puppets and telling a short story.

Make it work for your child

Offer pre-cut puppets or thicker handles. Your child can choose a character, make sound effects, move a puppet, or tell the story.

🌈 Watercolor Rainbow

Ages 2–12

Start with red, yellow, and blue. See how many new colors you can mix for your rainbow.

What you’ll need

Red, yellow, and blue watercolors, a brush or sponge, watercolor paper, and a cup of water.

How to play

  1. Paint a small patch of red, yellow, and blue on a corner of the paper.
  2. Mix red and yellow to make orange, yellow and blue to make green, and blue and red to make purple.
  3. Use your six colors to paint a rainbow.

Make it work for your child

Start with just two colors and mix one new shade together. Offer a sponge or a thick-handled brush, and say the color names aloud as you mix.

🍎 Fruit Stamping

Ages 2–5

What shape does an apple leave on paper? Try stamping with fruit to make prints and patterns.

What you’ll need

An apple or pear, washable paint, a paper plate, and paper. Have a grown-up cut the fruit in half.

How to play

  1. Pour a little paint onto the plate.
  2. Dip the cut side of the fruit into the paint and press it onto the paper.
  3. Lift the fruit to see your print. Try making a row of prints or alternating two colors.

Make it work for your child

Start with one color and tape the paper to the table if it slides. Your child can stamp with you or point to where they want the next print to go.

🍃 Nature Collage

Ages 2–8

Turn leaves and other finds from a nature walk into a picture.

What you’ll need

Fallen leaves, petals, or small twigs, plus construction paper, glue, and markers.

How to play

  1. Take a walk together and collect a few fallen leaves or other natural materials.
  2. Move your finds around on the paper until you like how they look.
  3. Glue them down and draw around them to finish your picture.

Make it work for your child

Offer two large leaves to choose from, or draw a path they can fill with leaves. Your child can point to where each piece goes while you handle the glue.

Monserrat’s tip

Try painting a background first. When it dries, add your leaves to make a garden or a forest.

🖍️ Watercolor Resist Art

Ages 2–8

Draw with a white crayon, then paint over the paper to reveal your hidden picture.

What you’ll need

A white crayon, watercolor paper, watercolor paint, a brush, and a cup of water.

How to play

  1. Press a white crayon firmly against the paper to draw a shape or write a secret message.
  2. Dip the brush into a color and paint across the drawing.
  3. Watch the white marks appear as the paint covers the paper around them.

Make it work for your child

Offer a thick crayon, tape the paper to the table, or draw a shape for your child to reveal. They can choose the paint color by pointing or telling you.

Monserrat’s tip

Make a secret picture for someone else to reveal.

🎨 Colorful Watercolor Silhouette

Ages 2–5

Make a dark paper shape stand out against a bright watercolor background.

What you’ll need

Black construction paper, watercolor paper, watercolors, a brush, a cup of water, a pencil, scissors, and glue.

How to play

  1. Draw a large shape on black paper, such as a cat, a leaf, or a house.
  2. Ask a grown-up to help cut it out, then glue it onto watercolor paper.
  3. Paint around the shape. Try a light color next to the dark edge and see how it stands out.

Make it work for your child

Offer two large, pre-cut shapes to choose from. Your child can point to where the shape goes, then paint with a sponge or a thick-handled brush.

Monserrat’s tip

Repeat the same shape across the page and paint a different color around each one.

🫧 Bubble Painting

Ages 2–12

Catch colorful bubbles on paper and see what patterns they leave behind.

What you’ll need

Bubble solution, washable paint, shallow cups, a bubble wand, white cardstock, and a towel to cover the table.

How to play

  1. Have a grown-up mix a little washable paint into a shallow cup of bubble solution.
  2. Ask the grown-up to blow bubbles toward the paper with the wand.
  3. Watch for rings where the bubbles pop. Try a second color and let the picture dry.

Make it work for your child

Let your child choose the colors, hold the paper flat, or watch the bubbles pop. Start with one color and a few bubbles at a time.

Monserrat’s tip

When the prints are dry, draw stems under the rings to turn them into a bubble garden.

🎨 Marble Painting

Ages 2–12

Tilt a box and watch a rolling ball leave colorful trails.

What you’ll need

A shallow cardboard box, paper, tape, washable paint, and a smooth, age-appropriate ball or a marble.

How to play

  1. Tape a sheet of paper inside the box.
  2. Dip the ball in a little paint and place it on the paper.
  3. Hold both sides of the box and tilt it gently to roll the ball.
  4. Try another color and see where the trails cross.

Make it work for your child

Start with one color. Hold the box together, or let your child point to which side to lift. For children under 3 or children who put objects in their mouths, use a large, age-appropriate ball and keep marbles and small balls out of reach.

Monserrat’s tip

Look at your finished trails. Do they remind you of rivers, roads, or something else?

Finger Painting Fun

Ages 2–12

Use your hands to make colorful shapes, patterns, and pictures.

What you’ll need

Non-toxic, washable finger paints, thick paper, a shallow tray, a table cover, and a damp cloth.

How to play

  1. Cover the table and pour a little paint onto the tray.
  2. Use your fingers or palms to make dots, lines, and shapes.
  3. Try mixing two colors. What new color do you see?
  4. Let your picture dry and wash your hands.

Make it work for your child

Start with two colors. Offer a sponge or brush if your child prefers to keep paint off their hands. They can also choose the colors and tell you where to paint.

Ice Painting

Ages 2–12

Slide colorful ice across paper and see the trails it leaves as it melts.

What you’ll need

An ice cube tray, water, food coloring, craft sticks, thick white paper, and a shallow tray or towel to catch drips.

How to play

  1. Have a grown-up mix a little food coloring into water and pour it into the ice cube tray.
  2. Add a craft stick to each cube for a handle, then freeze.
  3. With a grown-up nearby, hold a stick and glide the colored ice across the paper. Keep the cubes and sticks out of mouths.
  4. Try a second color and see what happens where the trails meet.

Make it work for your child

Tape the paper down so it stays put. If your child prefers to avoid the cold, they can choose a color and point to where they want you to move the ice.

Cardboard Guitar

Ages 3–10

Turn an empty tissue box into a guitar and listen to the sounds different rubber bands make.

What you’ll need

An empty tissue box, a cardboard paper towel tube, rubber bands of different thicknesses, tape, and markers or crayons.

How to play

  1. Have a grown-up remove the plastic film from the tissue box opening.
  2. Decorate the box and tape the cardboard tube to one end as the neck.
  3. Have a grown-up stretch the rubber bands around the box so they cross the opening.
  4. Pluck each band gently and listen. Which sounds higher or lower?

Make it work for your child

Start with two bands. Hold the box steady while your child plucks, or let them pick a band for you to play. They can also rest a hand on the side of the box to feel it vibrate.

Grown-up help — Keep loose rubber bands away from children who put objects in their mouths.

Tissue Paper Pom-Poms

Ages 5–8 and up

Fold and fluff layers of tissue paper to make a colorful decoration.

What you’ll need

8–10 sheets of tissue paper, a pipe cleaner, scissors, and string if you want to hang the pom-pom.

How to play

  1. Stack the tissue paper sheets and line up the edges.
  2. Fold the stack back and forth like an accordion.
  3. Have a grown-up secure the center with a pipe cleaner and trim the ends into rounded shapes.
  4. Fan out both sides and gently lift one layer at a time until the pom-pom looks full.
  5. If you want to hang it, ask a grown-up to attach the string and hang it out of children’s reach.

Make it work for your child

Try alternating two contrasting colors so the layers are easier to see. A grown-up can start the folds or separate the first layer. Your child can choose the colors, hold the stack, or fluff the paper.

Sliding-Bead Fidget Bracelet

Ages 3–12

Make a bracelet with a paper bead that slides back and forth.

What you’ll need

Cardstock, a wide strip of soft fabric, tape, and markers.

How to play

  1. Have a grown-up roll the cardstock into a wide tube and tape it closed to make one large paper bead.
  2. Thread the fabric strip through the bead and slide it from one end to the other.
  3. Try it around your wrist with grown-up help, or lay it flat on the table and slide the bead there.

Make it work for your child

Use a wider strip or hold one end steady while your child slides the bead. They can also choose the colors and decorate the paper.

Nature Weaving

Ages 3–12

Weave leaves and other flat nature finds through a simple cardboard loom.

What you’ll need

Cardboard, wide paper strips, tape, and flat leaves.

How to play

  1. Tape the ends of several paper strips across the cardboard, leaving space underneath each one.
  2. Slide a leaf over one strip and under the next.
  3. Add another leaf or paper strip and try starting in the opposite direction.

Make it work for your child

Use fewer, wider strips and start the first weave together. Your child can choose and place the leaves while you help with the weaving.

Ripped Paper Collage

Ages 2–12

Tear colorful paper and arrange the pieces into a picture.

What you’ll need

Colored paper, cardstock, and a glue stick.

How to play

  1. Tear colored paper into pieces of different shapes and sizes.
  2. Move the pieces around on the cardstock until you like the picture.
  3. Glue the pieces down, starting with the biggest ones.

Make it work for your child

Start each tear for your child or offer pre-torn pieces. Try two contrasting colors so the shapes stand out.

Color by Number

Ages 3–12

Use a simple color key to fill in a picture one section at a time.

What you’ll need

Paper, a pencil or marker, and crayons or colored pencils.

How to play

  1. Draw a picture with a few large sections, such as a flower or kite.
  2. Write a number in each section and make a key that matches each number to a color.
  3. Find the sections with the same number and color them using the key.

Make it work for your child

Start with two colors and mark each number in the key with a swatch. Say the numbers and colors aloud or point to them together.

Diamond Art

Ages 6–8

Make a mosaic with colorful paper diamonds.

What you’ll need

Cardstock, colored paper, scissors, and a glue stick.

How to play

  1. Draw a simple outline on cardstock, such as a star or heart.
  2. Ask a grown-up to help cut colorful paper into diamond shapes.
  3. Arrange the diamonds inside the outline and glue them in place.

Make it work for your child

Use large, pre-cut diamonds on a high-contrast background. Your child can point to where each piece should go.

Clothespin Painting & Transfers

Ages 3–5

Pick up sponge pieces with a clothespin and use them to stamp paint.

What you’ll need

A large clothespin, sponge pieces, washable paint, paper, and two shallow trays.

How to play

  1. Set sponge pieces in one tray and use the clothespin to move them to the other.
  2. Hold a sponge piece with the clothespin, dip it in paint, and press it onto paper.
  3. Try another color and see which shapes or patterns you can make.

Make it work for your child

Try a clothespin that opens easily, or let your child hold a larger sponge directly. You can steady the tray while they move the pieces.

Homemade Puzzles

Ages 3–5

Turn a favorite drawing into a puzzle with a few big pieces.

What you’ll need

A drawing or printed picture, cardboard, a glue stick, and scissors.

How to play

  1. Glue the picture to cardboard and let it dry.
  2. Have a grown-up cut it into two to four large pieces.
  3. Mix up the pieces and put the picture back together.

Make it work for your child

Start with two pieces and keep a copy of the whole picture nearby. Offer a plain background so the puzzle edges are easier to see.
