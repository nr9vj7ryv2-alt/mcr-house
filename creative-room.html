🍁 Leaf Rubbing Art Fall

Ages 3–12

Collect a few fallen leaves on a walk, place them under a sheet of paper, and rub a crayon sideways across the surface to reveal each leaf's veins and shape. A simple way to explore texture, patterns, and the outdoors together.

🎃 Pumpkin Patch Counting Fall

Ages 3–5

Using mini pumpkins (real or drawn), practice counting, sorting by size, or simple addition — "if we have 3 big pumpkins and 2 small ones, how many altogether?" A hands-on way to mix seasonal fun with early math.

Shadow Puppet Theater 🖨️ Printable

Ages 5–12

Make paper puppets and bring a story to life with light and shadows.

Download Puppet Cutouts Download Theater Instructions

What you’ll need

Printed puppet cutouts, scissors, tape, craft sticks, a flashlight, and a light-colored wall.

How to play

  1. Cut out the puppets with grown-up help and tape each one to a craft stick.
  2. Point the flashlight at the wall and hold a puppet between the light and the wall.
  3. Move the puppet closer to and farther from the light. Watch how its shadow changes.
  4. Take turns moving the puppets and telling a short story.

Make it work for your child

Offer pre-cut puppets or thicker handles. Your child can choose a character, make sound effects, move a puppet, or tell the story.

🌈 Watercolor Rainbow

Ages 2–12

Start with red, yellow, and blue. See how many new colors you can mix for your rainbow.

What you’ll need

Red, yellow, and blue watercolors, a brush or sponge, watercolor paper, and a cup of water.

How to play

  1. Paint a small patch of red, yellow, and blue on a corner of the paper.
  2. Mix red and yellow to make orange, yellow and blue to make green, and blue and red to make purple.
  3. Use your six colors to paint a rainbow.

Make it work for your child

Start with just two colors and mix one new shade together. Offer a sponge or a thick-handled brush, and say the color names aloud as you mix.

🍎 Fruit Stamping

Ages 2–5

What shape does an apple leave on paper? Try stamping with fruit to make prints and patterns.

What you’ll need

An apple or pear, washable paint, a paper plate, and paper. Have a grown-up cut the fruit in half.

How to play

  1. Pour a little paint onto the plate.
  2. Dip the cut side of the fruit into the paint and press it onto the paper.
  3. Lift the fruit to see your print. Try making a row of prints or alternating two colors.

Make it work for your child

Start with one color and tape the paper to the table if it slides. Your child can stamp with you or point to where they want the next print to go.

🍃 Nature Collage

Ages 2–8

Turn leaves and other finds from a nature walk into a picture.

What you’ll need

Fallen leaves, petals, or small twigs, plus construction paper, glue, and markers.

How to play

  1. Take a walk together and collect a few fallen leaves or other natural materials.
  2. Move your finds around on the paper until you like how they look.
  3. Glue them down and draw around them to finish your picture.

Make it work for your child

Offer two large leaves to choose from, or draw a path they can fill with leaves. Your child can point to where each piece goes while you handle the glue.

Monserrat’s tip

Try painting a background first. When it dries, add your leaves to make a garden or a forest.

🖍️ Watercolor Resist Art

Ages 2–8

Draw with a white crayon, then paint over the paper to reveal your hidden picture.

What you’ll need

A white crayon, watercolor paper, watercolor paint, a brush, and a cup of water.

How to play

  1. Press a white crayon firmly against the paper to draw a shape or write a secret message.
  2. Dip the brush into a color and paint across the drawing.
  3. Watch the white marks appear as the paint covers the paper around them.

Make it work for your child

Offer a thick crayon, tape the paper to the table, or draw a shape for your child to reveal. They can choose the paint color by pointing or telling you.

Monserrat’s tip

Make a secret picture for someone else to reveal.

🎨 Colorful Watercolor Silhouette

Ages 2–5

Make a dark paper shape stand out against a bright watercolor background.

What you’ll need

Black construction paper, watercolor paper, watercolors, a brush, a cup of water, a pencil, scissors, and glue.

How to play

  1. Draw a large shape on black paper, such as a cat, a leaf, or a house.
  2. Ask a grown-up to help cut it out, then glue it onto watercolor paper.
  3. Paint around the shape. Try a light color next to the dark edge and see how it stands out.

Make it work for your child

Offer two large, pre-cut shapes to choose from. Your child can point to where the shape goes, then paint with a sponge or a thick-handled brush.

Monserrat’s tip

Repeat the same shape across the page and paint a different color around each one.

🫧 Bubble Painting

Ages 2–12

Catch colorful bubbles on paper and see what patterns they leave behind.

What you’ll need

Bubble solution, washable paint, shallow cups, a bubble wand, white cardstock, and a towel to cover the table.

How to play

  1. Have a grown-up mix a little washable paint into a shallow cup of bubble solution.
  2. Ask the grown-up to blow bubbles toward the paper with the wand.
  3. Watch for rings where the bubbles pop. Try a second color and let the picture dry.

Make it work for your child

Let your child choose the colors, hold the paper flat, or watch the bubbles pop. Start with one color and a few bubbles at a time.

Monserrat’s tip

When the prints are dry, draw stems under the rings to turn them into a bubble garden.

🎨 Marble Painting

Ages 2–12

Tilt a box and watch a rolling ball leave colorful trails.

What you’ll need

A shallow cardboard box, paper, tape, washable paint, and a smooth, age-appropriate ball or a marble.

How to play

  1. Tape a sheet of paper inside the box.
  2. Dip the ball in a little paint and place it on the paper.
  3. Hold both sides of the box and tilt it gently to roll the ball.
  4. Try another color and see where the trails cross.

Make it work for your child

Start with one color. Hold the box together, or let your child point to which side to lift. For children under 3 or children who put objects in their mouths, use a large, age-appropriate ball and keep marbles and small balls out of reach.

Monserrat’s tip

Look at your finished trails. Do they remind you of rivers, roads, or something else?

Finger Painting Fun

Ages 2–12

Use your hands to make colorful shapes, patterns, and pictures.

What you’ll need

Washable finger paints, thick white paper, apron or old shirt, baby wipes or damp cloth, newspaper or table cover.

How to play

  1. Cover the work area.
  2. Pour small amounts of washable paint onto a tray.
  3. Use your hands to make shapes and patterns.
  4. Try mixing colors or making a picture.
  5. Let the artwork dry.

Make it work for your child

Offer a sponge or a brush, or start with two colors.

Grown-up help: Use only non-toxic, washable finger paints. Adult supervision is recommended, especially with younger children. Wash hands thoroughly after the activity.

Ice Painting

Ages 2–12

Slide colorful ice across paper and see the trails it leaves as it melts.

What you’ll need

Ice cube tray, water, washable paint or food coloring, popsicle sticks (optional), white paper or cardstock, tray or towel.

How to play

  1. Pour colored water into an ice cube tray.
  2. Add popsicle sticks if you want handles.
  3. Freeze the water.
  4. Set the cubes on a tray.
  5. Slide the cubes across the paper.
  6. Watch the ice melt.
  7. Let the picture dry.

Make it work for your child

Offer a paint stick handle or lightweight gloves if cold feels uncomfortable.

Grown-up help: Use only non-toxic coloring materials. Dry any wet floors immediately to prevent slipping. Adult supervision is recommended.

Cardboard Guitar

Ages 3–10

Turn a cardboard box into a guitar and compare the sounds the bands make.

What you’ll need

Empty tissue box or cereal box, cardboard paper towel tube, 4–6 rubber bands of varying thicknesses, scissors, tape or glue, pencil or craft stick.

How to play

  1. Remove the plastic film from the box opening.
  2. Decorate the box if you like.
  3. Stretch rubber bands across the opening with adult help.
  4. Slide a pencil or craft stick under the bands as a bridge.
  5. Ask an adult to cut a hole for the tube.
  6. Attach the tube to make the neck.
  7. Pluck the bands gently and compare the sounds.

Make it work for your child

Let your child decorate the box or choose the order to pluck the bands.

Grown-up help: An adult handles sharp tools and hot glue. Keep loose rubber bands out of reach of children who may mouth them.

Tissue Paper Pom-Poms

Ages 5–8 and up

Fold and fluff layers of tissue paper to make a colorful decoration.

What you’ll need

8–10 sheets of tissue paper, floral wire or a pipe cleaner, sharp scissors for adult use, hanging string.

How to play

  1. Stack the sheets of tissue paper.
  2. Fold them back and forth like an accordion.
  3. Have an adult secure the center with wire or a pipe cleaner.
  4. Ask the adult to round or point the ends.
  5. Fan out both sides.
  6. Gently lift each layer to make a ball.
  7. Have an adult tie on the hanging string.

Make it work for your child

Try contrasting colors to make the layers easier to see, or have an adult separate the first layer.

Grown-up help: An adult handles sharp scissors and wire and hangs the finished decoration out of children’s reach.
